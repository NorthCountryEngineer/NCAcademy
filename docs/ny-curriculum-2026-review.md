# New York curriculum for 2026 and 2027

NCAcademy’s New York planning catalog covers **Pre-K, kindergarten and grades 1–12**, with the academic portions of every NYSED subject area represented. The current instructional year is **2026–2027**. Calendar-year 2026 assessment changes are included below so a student’s examination version can be distinguished from the standards used for current instruction. Review date: **October 18, 2026**.

New York establishes learning standards, frameworks and instructional requirements; schools develop their own curriculum and select materials. A subject appearing at several grades means it is available for appropriate school placement, enrichment or integrated instruction. It does not mean that every school must offer that named course in every listed grade. [NYSED curriculum guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/early-learning/curriculum), [NYSED standards by content area](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/nys-p-12-learning-standards-content-area).

The accompanying [coverage table](ny-curriculum-2026-coverage.csv) gives every supported grade/subject combination, its topics, framework editions, limitations and source links. The [subject and source register](ny-curriculum-2026-register.md) lists the complete catalog, implementation transitions and source-review evidence. Together they distinguish statewide expectations, proficiency-based pathways, cross-curricular support and school-selected electives.

## Coverage and teaching readiness

The catalog represents **tutoring scope**, including reading and writing instruction, mathematics, science, history and civics, languages, computing, finance, health, career theory and academic arts. It includes music literacy, theory and history; it excludes band rehearsal and instrumental performance assessment. The same boundary applies to sports performance, clinical practice, machinery operation, physical dance execution and supervised laboratory or safety competencies.

Most entries are reviewed framework and topic plans. They still need exact standard mappings, a school-approved sequence and suitable teaching material before becoming an approved AI-teacher corpus. The existing Earth and Space Sciences and Environmental Science packages remain explicitly **partial** mappings with limited teaching summaries. Their readiness does not establish full coverage of those courses. [Existing science package review](ny-school-curriculum-sources.md).

School setup can select all grades and subjects, inspect the resulting plans and create selected classes in batches of at most 100. Running a plan does not create every possible elective or authorize an AI teacher. New reference plans and their knowledge snapshots show the missing alignment and materials; a source index alone cannot be approved as a ready teaching corpus. Teacher behavior, model use, external-source review and human publication controls remain separate.

## Mathematics

Use the **2017 Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards, updated June 2019**. Preserve each PK–8 grade’s content and mathematical practices. High-school pathways include Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II. Tutoring should develop understanding, representations, mathematical explanation, modeling and error analysis while keeping assessed work student-authored.

Precalculus, calculus, statistics/probability, discrete mathematics and applied mathematics are represented as local electives. The state’s Plus standards support advanced preparation but do not constitute a course or a complete calculus/statistics syllabus. AP, IB and college courses require their own current framework or authorized local syllabus. [Mathematics standards](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/nys-next-generation-mathematics-p-12-standards.pdf).

## Literacy and humanities

ELA includes foundational reading in the early grades, vocabulary, comprehension, literary/informational analysis, language conventions, research, discussion, presentation and writing instruction. Students can receive help planning, revising and evaluating their own writing; an agent cannot write their submission. High-school ELA uses **9–10 and 11–12 bands**, rather than four invented annual standards sets. [ELA resources](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/ela-guidance-resources).

Social studies includes history, geography, economics, civics and the practices of evaluating evidence, reasoning about time and causation, comparing contexts and considering civic participation. The framework’s grade sequence is preserved in the table. Local psychology, sociology, philosophy, creative writing, journalism and speech/debate courses are also represented, with an explicit requirement for a reviewed syllabus. [Social studies guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/social-studies-faq).

Research, library and media literacy extend across grades and subjects. Disciplinary reading/writing support has distinct **6–8, 9–10 and 11–12** standard tables; the source introduction’s broader high-school grouping does not erase those tables. Library/information instruction in grades 7 and 8 can be integrated with coursework. The media-literacy toolkit is guidance, not a newly adopted numbered standards set. [Disciplinary literacy standards](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/nys-next-generation-literacy-standards-grades-6-12.pdf), [school library guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/school-library-services-faq), [media literacy toolkit](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/media-literacy-toolkit.pdf).

## Science and engineering

Use New York’s **2016 P–12 Science Learning Standards**, including New York additions, scientific/engineering practices and crosscutting concepts. PK–5 has grade-specific expectations. Grades 6–8 share a middle-school band; a school chooses the annual sequence. High school uses course maps over the high-school band, rather than a mandated Biology-at-9/Environmental-Science-at-10 schedule. [Science guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/science), [science FAQ](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/science-faq).

High-school tutoring covers Biology; Earth and Space Sciences; Chemistry; Physics; and Environmental Science. The broader scope includes organism/cellular systems, heredity and evolution, ecosystems, matter and reactions, forces and energy, waves, astronomy, geologic history, Earth processes, weather, climate, resources and engineering. Local extensions include astronomy, anatomy/physiology, marine science, forensic science and other cataloged scientific or engineering electives. A school-defined environmental course can combine ecology, climate, cycles, pollution, resource use and sustainability without claiming a separate statewide tenth-grade syllabus. [Biology map](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/biology.pdf), [Earth and Space Sciences map](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/ess.pdf), [Chemistry map](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/chemistry.pdf), [Physics map](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/physics.pdf).

Concept explanations, experimental design and interpreting permitted data are tutorable. Secure Regents Investigations and teacher answer materials cannot enter a shared public corpus. Required Investigations remain authentic hands-on work; prerecorded demonstrations do not replace them. Separately, the general 1,200-minute laboratory policy permits a combination of hands-on and simulated/virtual experiences with satisfactory documented reports. A tutoring conversation does not itself award laboratory credit. [September 2026 Investigation FAQ](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/state-assessment/faq-regents-science-investigations-updated-9-26.pdf), [laboratory guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/science-guidance-resources).

## Computing and digital fluency

The **2020 Computer Science and Digital Fluency standards** use K–1, 2–3, 4–6, 7–8 and 9–12 bands. All K–12 grades are represented. Computing includes algorithms/programming, data/modeling, systems/networks, cybersecurity and societal effects. Digital fluency includes research, evaluating information, creation, collaboration, safe participation and appropriate use of tools. These can be taught within other courses; catalog availability does not establish a separate required annual class. [CSDF standards and implementation](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/computer-science-and-digital-fluency), [CSDF FAQ](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/computer-science-digital-literacy-faq).

Technology/engineering includes design reasoning, materials and process choices, systems, computer-aided design and technological effects. Advanced programming/data-science and engineering electives need locally approved scope. The historical MST technology framework must not replace current mathematics or science standards. AI literacy may supplement current instruction; the 2020 standard is not relabeled as a 2026 AI syllabus. [Technology standards](https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/technology-education-learning-standards).

## Languages and multilingual learning

Modern languages, classical languages and American Sign Language have separate pathways. Use the **2021 World Languages standards**, their communication/culture expectations and Checkpoints A, B and C. Language, checkpoint, proficiency and prerequisites must be selected for the school’s course; grade alone does not determine them. The modern/classical communication strands use different numbering. Schools may offer elementary or heritage-language instruction without a statewide requirement that every listed language start at a fixed grade. [World Languages standards and requirements](https://www.nysed.gov/world-languages/standards-and-graduation-requirements).

ASL tutoring requires visual/signing access and qualified review; text alone cannot establish signing proficiency. Current source guidance contains an ASL proficiency-range inconsistency, recorded in the register rather than resolved by an invented target. The themes PDF lists 17 topics; conflicting webpage counts are not used to invent an eighteenth topic. [World Languages guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/world-languages/instructional-guidance), [themes and topics PDF](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/world-languages/nys-wl-themes-and-topics-2021.pdf).

English-language development and home-language/bilingual literacy support span the academic curriculum. Use New York’s **2023 Academic and Linguistic Demands** alongside grade-level content and approved language scaffolds. The move to WIDA assessments does **not** replace New York’s instructional framework with the WIDA 2020 ELD standards. [Academic and Linguistic Demands](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/bilingual-ed/academic-and-linguistic-demands-final-11-7-23-a.pdf), [NYSED WIDA transition](https://www.nysed.gov/state-assessment/wida).

## Health finance and career studies

Health covers age-appropriate wellness, nutrition, mental-health literacy, prevention, relationships, safety and decision-making. Family and Consumer Sciences includes consumer choices, food/nutrition, development/families, housing/textiles and independent living. Use the current health/FACS standards with later instructional guidance and required-topic overlays; do not use their combined historical PDF as the current PE standard. **Physical Education’s current standards are the 2020 edition**, fully implemented from fall 2023. Its knowledge and planning components are included; movement competence remains practical. [Health education](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/health-education), [FACS standards](https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/facs-learning-standards), [PE guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/physical-education-guidance-resources).

Age-appropriate HIV/AIDS instruction belongs within sequential K–6 health and required health courses in the 7–8 and 9–12 bands. The 2025 guidance leaves curriculum selection and applicable prevention-method parent exemptions to school procedures; it does not establish a universal comprehensive-sexuality course. The catalog records this overlay but does not implement exemption management. [NYSED HIV/AIDS guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/student-support-services/hiv-aids-guidance.pdf).

Personal finance includes age-appropriate money decisions, budgeting, income, credit/debt, saving/investing, risk and consumer protection. The **March 2026 instructional requirement** begins in **2026–27 for the 5–8 and 9–12 bands**, with instruction completed by each band’s end. **K–4 begins in 2027–28**. It does not create a new statewide standalone graduation credit or a new standards set. The guidance objectives and local placement remain separate from those requirements. [Personal finance FAQ](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/personal-finance-education-faq).

Career development includes planning, employability, applied academics, information and systems. Middle-level CTE spans the seven currently listed content areas, despite an outdated “six” in introductory page prose. Academic high-school CTE pathways include business/marketing, accounting, entrepreneurship, agriculture/food systems, health sciences, human development, nutrition, housing/textiles and trade/technical theory. Their appearance is not a universal requirement to take each named elective. Clinical practice, tool operation, occupational certification and supervised practical competencies require the appropriate human program. [CDOS](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/career-development-and-occupational-studies), [middle-level CTE](https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/new-york-state-middle-level-cte-requirement), [CTE programs](https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/program-content).

Cross-curricular required topics include applicable citizenship/Dignity Act and internet-safety instruction, New York history/civics, substance-abuse education, highway/fire/life safety, humane treatment and conservation. Erin’s Law and senior-high CPR/AED instruction retain their age, content and practical boundaries. These are instructional overlays, not additional invented annual courses or a promise that chat satisfies every requirement. [NYSED general instructional requirements](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/general-school-requirements-related-standards-and-instruction-section-1002), [Erin’s Law guidance](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/erins-law).

## Academic arts

The **2017 Arts standards** cover visual arts, music, media arts, theatre and dance. Include interpretation, literacy, history/context, criticism, design/planning and reflection. Preserve the processes of creating, performing/presenting/producing, responding and connecting, while distinguishing what an agent can support from what requires practical demonstration. PK–8 expectations are grade-specific; high-school **Proficient, Accomplished and Advanced** levels are not assigned automatically to grades 9, 10, 11 and 12. [NYSED arts standards](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/arts).

Visual-art analysis, music notation/theory, media analysis, dramatic texts and dance history/choreographic analysis remain represented. Instrumental/ensemble rehearsal, vocal or physical technique, staged performance and performance proficiency cannot be certified through text tutoring. Practical portions can link to human-led coursework without implying that the application teaches or assesses them completely.

## Accessibility and individualized pathways

Accessible text, alternative representations, language support, prerequisites and flexible pacing apply across the catalog. Student preferences can guide presentation; they do not establish disability, ability, language eligibility or a permanent learning-style diagnosis. A school-approved individualized plan governs accommodations and specialized instruction.

NYSAA uses Dynamic Learning Maps for eligible students in ELA, mathematics and science, with reduced depth, breadth and complexity. An alternate pathway needs a separately reviewed Essential Elements mapping and school-authorized placement. This catalog does not claim that mapping or substitute a younger grade automatically. Assessment eligibility is not determined by an agent. [NYSED alternate assessment](https://www.nysed.gov/state-assessment/new-york-state-alternate-assessment).

## Source maintenance and verification

Four explicit local JSON bundles hold subject scope, grade applicability, actual framework editions, topic summaries, limitations, transitions and source references. Each source distinguishes an official page/document review from an exact downloaded-byte review. Exact-byte reviews carry SHA-256 and size; page reviews do not invent a byte hash. The source register preserves that distinction. No full standards PDF, secure investigation, textbook, answer bank or third-party media is redistributed with the catalog.

Runtime planning uses these bounded local bundles and makes no live fetch or model call. A changed review changes the plan and reference-snapshot hashes. Source-review validity ends **June 30, 2027**; a later year needs a new applicability review even if the underlying standards edition stays unchanged. Another state can reuse the subject schema and planner through an explicitly registered adapter with its own jurisdiction, official source/publisher policy and applicability dates. The present New York loader deliberately accepts only registered NYSED bundles; adding an arbitrary file is not installation. A custom course needs a separately reviewed syllabus and teaching package; the reserved custom-ingestion UI does not parse arbitrary uploads.

This revision is prepared locally. Its release image marker is unresolved because the previously deployed API image does not contain the new catalog. A matching immutable image and the normal API-first release checks are required before deployment. The corpus evidence shape changes, including for the two partial science packages, so existing affected teaching approvals need a refreshed snapshot and human review before use. No new database schema migration is introduced.

Run the coverage-report check and the curriculum/source tests described in [verification](verification.md) after editing the review. Before approving a course for tutoring, complete its exact alignment, instructional materials, local sequence, access classification, external-source decisions and human review. Adding a topic to a planning catalog never relaxes the application’s student-work or publication rules.
