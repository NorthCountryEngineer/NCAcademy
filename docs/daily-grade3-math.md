# Daily Grade 3 mathematics practice

The **Daily math practice** tab is available for a class with the exact New York Grade 3 mathematics 2026–2027 alignment. It uses the [NYSED Next Generation standards](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/mathematics-guidance-resources), the installed [Grade 3 teaching package](ny-grade3-mathematics.md), and a separate NCAcademy question bank. The bank includes **44 selectable content skills and nine unit mixes**. Every question maps to one or more standards and an approved lesson; the eight Mathematical Practices appear as reflection prompts. Scores cover the structured answers. Explaining reasoning, fluency over time, and demonstrating a drawing or tool still benefit from a human review. This is formative practice with reviewed examples, so it is not designed for a closed-book assessment.

1. In **Courses → Mathematics (grade 3) → Daily math practice**, select an enrolled NCAcademy student, a date, the topic being taught in school, and the number of questions. Dates follow New York time. Future assignments are hidden from the student until their scheduled day.
2. Choose **Generate assignment preview**. Review the exact generated problems and links before selecting **Publish this assignment**. Generate a new preview for another version. Drafts remain in the staff history; publication records the exact reviewed questions. A model is never called to write, publish, score or reuse the assignment.
3. The student opens the class and starts under **Today’s assignment**. They enter their own answers and submit once. The server marks each answer and opens the first approved lesson linked to a missed question. Other recommended lessons are available from the same result. A perfect score has no required review lesson.
4. Use **Ask the lesson teacher** from a recommended lesson, or open **Lesson Q&A** to ask about a math concept. The current human-approved managed teacher uses reviewed class explanations and fixed coaching questions, with source citations and saved conversation history. A follow-up can stay with the same lesson. The practice chat does not send answers, questions, or student information to a model provider and does not compute answers to assignments. Existing provider-backed tutoring on other courses is unchanged.
5. In **Assignment history**, open a published assignment or completed result and select **Reuse exact questions**. Choose a student and date, create a new preview, and publish when ready. The copy retains the exact variant and standards mapping without carrying over answers or scores. Old submissions remain readable in their original records.

Only current human staff who can manage the class may preview, publish or review assignments. Only the assigned, currently enrolled local student can open and submit their published work. Students cannot see other students’ assignments, drafts, future work or stored answer keys. Staff can review a saved attempt but cannot submit it as the student. School placement and enrollment revocation take effect on the next request. Changing the approved corpus makes the associated preview or pending submission unavailable; historical grades remain recorded and no longer admitted lesson text is withheld. Staff can review and reassign a copy against the newly approved material.

Schema 17 adds immutable encrypted assignment, publication and result records. The independent family PII key protects exact questions and answer keys, student answers and practice chat. AES-256-GCM authenticates the tenant, record, type, course and owner scope. Database constraints reject mutation to the historical records. Assignment creation, publication, submission and authorized replay add hash-chain audit events; the metadata outbox does not deliver private answers or chats to Core. Repeated identical submissions return the original result. A different second submission is rejected. Stored records and audits are included in normal backup/preservation verification.

For acceptance, use a synthetic local account and the approved exact Grade 3 corpus and teacher. Generate two previews for one skill and confirm the variants differ; publish only the reviewed one. Sign in as the student, answer one question incorrectly, verify the matched lesson opens and ask its teacher a follow-up. Reload to verify the result and transcript survive. Sign back in as staff, reuse the original and compare prompts and choices; the new preview has no result. Confirm that drafts, future work and other learners’ attempts stay private, and that a changed corpus withholds stale text. Use the existing tests for all grades and permissions outside this course. No real student response or external model is needed for acceptance.
